Missed Open Enrollment? How a Special Enrollment Period Can Still Get You Covered

Blog / Special Enrollment Periods

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

Open enrollment for 2027 coverage starts November 1 and runs through January 15. But here's something a lot of people don't know: open enrollment isn't the only time you can get health insurance. If something big changes in your life, you may qualify for a Special Enrollment Period — a window that lets you sign up for coverage even though open enrollment is closed. The catch is that the window is short, and most people only find out about it after it's closed.

What Counts As A Qualifying Life Event?

You can't just decide mid-year that you want a different plan. You need a qualifying event, and the common ones fall into a few groups.

Losing coverage. This is the big one. Losing job-based insurance (including when COBRA runs out), aging off a parent's plan at 26, losing Medicaid or CHIP, or having your plan discontinued all qualify. Cancelling your own coverage on purpose doesn't count.

Changes in your household. Getting married, having a baby, adopting a child, or a divorce or death that causes you to lose coverage.

Moving. A move to a new ZIP code or county that changes which plans are available to you counts. Moving just for medical treatment or a vacation doesn't.

Other situations. Certain changes like being offered an HRA or QSEHRA from an employer, getting released from incarceration, or being hit by a natural disaster can also open a window.

The 60-Day Clock

For most of these events, you have 60 days to pick a plan. That sounds like plenty of time until life gets busy. If you lose job-based coverage, for example, a Marketplace plan can start the first day of the month after your coverage ends — but only if you apply within that 60-day window. Miss it, and you're generally waiting until the next open enrollment.

Timing also depends on the event. If you get married, pick a plan by the last day of the month and coverage can start the first day of the next month. With a new baby, coverage can start the day of the birth as long as you enroll within the 60 days.

Losing Your Job? You Have Two Main Options

If you lose employer coverage, you can usually either enroll in a Marketplace plan or stay on your old plan through COBRA, which generally lasts up to 18 months. With COBRA you pay the full premium plus an administrative fee, which surprises a lot of people. A Marketplace plan may come with premium tax credits depending on your income, so it's worth comparing both before you decide.

Expect To Prove It

You may be asked to send documents confirming your qualifying event, like a letter showing the date your coverage ended. Keep your termination notice, marriage certificate, or proof of your new address handy, because it can save you a lot of back-and-forth.

The short version: if your life just changed, don't wait. Start the clock the day it happens, gather your paperwork, and get your options in front of you before the 60 days run out.
