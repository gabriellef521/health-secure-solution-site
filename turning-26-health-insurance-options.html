Turning 26? The Only Guide You Need to Know Your Health Insurance Options

Blog / Individuals & Families

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

Turning 26 means you're leaving your parents' health insurance. Don't panic. It happens to everyone, and it's easy to handle if you start early.

Your Options

Employer coverage

If your employer offers health insurance, this is usually very affordable, because your employer pays a contribution toward the cost. You can usually sign up when you lose your parents' plan, even if it's not their open enrollment. Ask HR how long you have.

Obamacare (the Marketplace)

If your income is lower, you can get what's called a subsidy. That's just money from the government that lowers your monthly bill, and the lower your income, the more help you get. If your job offers coverage, you usually won't get a subsidy. The other big thing: everyone gets accepted, and pre-existing conditions are covered. So if you have a health condition, this is usually the way to go. Losing your parents' plan also gives you a 60-day special enrollment period. You have 60 days after you lose coverage to pick a plan.

Private plans

Private plans are a great option if you're healthy or don't qualify for much of a subsidy. Approval is based on your health history, and you can apply any time of year. They're typically cheaper than Marketplace plans without a subsidy, and they can come with stronger coverage if you're approved. Here's how Marketplace and private plans compare.

Medicaid

Medicaid is free or very low cost health coverage from your state for people with low income. In states that expanded Medicaid, a single adult making under about $20,000 a year can usually qualify. Rules are different in every state. You can apply any time of year.

Before You Pick

Check that your doctors and prescriptions are covered by the new plan. Here's how to check. Then run through this quick list:

  • Your doctors are in the plan's network
  • Your prescriptions are covered
