Employer Coverage Too Expensive for Your Family? You May Have Another Option

Blog / Individuals & Families

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

Employer coverage went up again. Higher premiums, higher deductibles, and the biggest jump is usually when you add your spouse or kids. If that's your situation, you might be paying a lot more than you need to, and there's an option that a lot of people don't know about.

Your Whole Family Doesn't Have To Be On The Same Plan

This is the big one. A lot of people assume everyone has to go on the employer plan together. In reality, you can often keep yourself on your job's plan, since your own coverage is usually the affordable part, and get separate coverage for your spouse and kids. Done right, a split policy can save you thousands of dollars a year in some cases, while still giving everyone the coverage they need.

Which option is best for your family depends on their health. Here's how I think about it.

Ongoing Conditions Or Expensive Medication? Look At The ACA Marketplace

If someone in your family has an ongoing major condition, needs regular treatment, or takes expensive medication, take a look at the ACA Marketplace. ACA plans are guaranteed issue, which means they can't turn you down, and they cover pre-existing conditions and ongoing treatment.

Relatively Healthy Family? Check Out Private Insurance

If your family is relatively healthy and doesn't use their coverage a lot, private plans are worth a look. They're based on medical history, and if you qualify, they can offer lower rates, lower deductibles, and PPO options. These are still real insurance plans that will cover you if someone has a big claim. The trade-off is that approval is based on health history, so they aren't available to everyone.

Don't Wait Too Long

Marketplace open enrollment for 2027 runs November 1 through January 15. Select a plan by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1, or by January 15 for coverage that starts February 1. Your employer's open enrollment window may be shorter, so check your own deadline too.

The short version: your family doesn't have to be on the same plan. If adding them to your job plan costs too much, a split policy could save you a lot and still give everyone the coverage they need.
