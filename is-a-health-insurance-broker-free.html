Is Working With a Health Insurance Broker Free? How Brokers Get Paid

Blog / Working With A Broker

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

Short answer: yes. It doesn't cost you anything extra to work with a health insurance broker. If you've ever wondered what the catch is, here's how it actually works.

How Brokers Get Paid

When you enroll in a plan through a broker, the insurance company pays the broker a commission. That commission is already built into the plan's price, which means you pay the same premium whether you go through a broker or sign up on your own. For Marketplace (ACA) plans in particular, the plan and the price are the same either way. You don't get a separate bill from your broker.

So the real question isn't "what does it cost?" It's "what do I get for it?"

What A Good Broker Does For You

Compares both markets. Most people only look at one place. A broker can put Marketplace plans next to private plans, so you can see your real options side by side instead of guessing which route is better for you.

Checks the details that decide your costs. Are your doctors in the network? Are your prescriptions covered? Do you qualify for a tax credit that lowers your monthly premium? Those answers change which plan is actually the best deal, and they're easy to miss when you're clicking through a website alone.

Helps you enroll the right way. Deadlines, applications, and income questions can be confusing. A broker helps you get through them correctly so you don't end up with a gap in coverage.

Stays with you after you enroll. This is the part people don't expect. If a claim gets denied, a doctor turns out to be out of network, or your life changes mid-year, you have a real person to call instead of waiting on hold with a call center.

Questions To Ask Before You Pick A Broker

Not every broker works the same way, so it's fair to ask a few things up front. Which carriers can you quote? Will you compare Marketplace and private plans, or only one? Will you help me after I enroll, or only during enrollment? A good advisor will answer plainly and won't pressure you into a plan.

What A Broker Can't Do

A broker can't change what a plan costs, and no one can promise you the lowest price for every situation. What a broker can do is make sure you're looking at all of your options, so the plan you choose actually fits your doctors, your prescriptions, and your budget.

Why It Matters Right Now

Open enrollment for 2027 coverage starts November 1 and runs through January 15. That's the window when most people can pick a new plan or change the one they have. It's also when brokers get busy, so reaching out early gives you time to compare options without feeling rushed.

The short version: working with a broker is free to you, and a good one compares both markets and makes sure you're in the best spot possible. If you want help, you can reach out any time.
