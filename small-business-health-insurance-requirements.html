Do Small Businesses Have to Offer Health Insurance? What Owners Should Know

Blog / Small Business Health Insurance Requirements

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

If you're running a small business, chances are someone has told you that you're "required" to offer health insurance once you hire employees. For most small businesses, that's simply not true. But that doesn't mean skipping it is the right call — here's what's actually going on.

The Short Answer: No, Not for Most Small Businesses

Generally speaking, smaller employers aren't required to offer health coverage to their team. Larger employers can face different rules, so if your business is growing quickly, it's worth checking in on where you stand — but if you're a typical small business or a solo owner, you're not breaking any rules by not offering a plan.

So Why Offer It Anyway?

Because "not required" and "not worth it" are two very different things. Offering group health insurance is one of the most effective ways a small business can compete with larger companies for talent — it helps you attract and retain higher-caliber employees who might otherwise take a job somewhere with better benefits, even at similar pay.

You Don't Need a Big Team to Qualify

One of the most common misconceptions is that group coverage is only for companies with dozens of employees. In reality, some small business plans only require a single employee to qualify — including plans built specifically for owner-only businesses. If you've been holding off because you assumed you were "too small," it's worth a second look.

How Much of the Premium Do You Have to Cover?

There's no single required amount — that's entirely up to you as the employer. A common standard many small businesses land on is covering around 50% of the employee's premium, but plenty of owners choose to cover more (or less) depending on their budget and what makes sense for their team.

What About Just Covering Yourself?

If you're a business owner without employees yet, you're not out of options either — there are plans built specifically for owner-only or very small businesses, so you can get coverage for yourself and your family without needing a full team in place first.
