Healthy and Self-Employed? You May Have Better Options Outside the ACA

Blog / Self-Employed

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

Let me guess. You work for yourself, you're relatively healthy, and every month you pay a lot for health insurance you barely use. You go to a checkup, maybe fill a prescription, and that's about it. If that sounds like you, this one's worth reading before you hit auto-renew.

Why The ACA Marketplace Isn't Always The Best Fit

The ACA Marketplace is a great option for a lot of people. If you have a lot of health care needs, it's built for you, because plans have to cover you no matter your health history. And if your income is low enough, you can get help paying the premium. But if you're a relatively healthy business owner who doesn't use your coverage much and doesn't qualify for that help, you can end up paying for coverage designed to work for everyone, including people who use their insurance all the time.

Plans That Look At Your Health History

Here's what a lot of people don't know. Outside the ACA, there are plans that are based on your medical history. When you're relatively healthy, that works in your favor. In many cases that can mean lower rates, lower deductibles, and a PPO you can use anywhere in the country. A lot of people see savings of 30-40% on premiums with these plans.

So Who Might They Fit?

Usually someone who's relatively healthy and doesn't use their coverage a lot, and who doesn't qualify for much help with Marketplace premiums. These are still real insurance plans, so if you do end up with a big claim, they're there to cover it. If that sounds like you, it's worth comparing both side by side.

Now, these plans are not a fit for everyone. If you have ongoing major medical needs, a planned procedure, or regular specialist care, stay with the ACA Marketplace.

Don't Forget The Marketplace Deadlines

If the Marketplace ends up being your best fit, open enrollment for 2027 runs November 1 through January 15. Select a plan by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1, or by January 15 for coverage that starts February 1.

The short version: if you're healthy and self-employed, you may have better options outside the ACA, but they're not right for everyone. The only way to know is to compare both.
