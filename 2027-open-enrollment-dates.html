When Is Open Enrollment for 2027 Health Insurance? Key Dates You Need to Know

Blog / 2027 Open Enrollment Dates

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

If you buy your own health insurance — whether through the Marketplace or a private plan tied to open enrollment — there's one date range you need on your calendar every year. Miss it, and you're generally stuck waiting until the following year unless you qualify for a special enrollment period. Here's exactly when open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs, the one deadline that matters most, and a change this year that's worth understanding before you enroll.

The 2027 Open Enrollment Window

For most states, including Florida, open enrollment for 2027 health coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Florida doesn't run its own exchange — every Florida ACA application, plan comparison, and enrollment happens through the federal marketplace at HealthCare.gov, so these are the dates that apply here.

Outside of this window, you generally can't enroll in or change a Marketplace plan unless a qualifying life event opens up a special enrollment period — something we'll cover in a separate article, since it trips up more people than you'd expect.

The Date That Actually Matters Most: December 15, 2026

The full window runs into mid-January, but if you want your new plan active on January 1, 2027 — with no gap in coverage — you need to enroll by December 15, 2026. Enroll after that and your coverage won't start until February 1, which can matter a lot if you have appointments, prescriptions, or procedures planned early in the year.

In practice, this means the real deadline for most people isn't January 15 — it's December 15. Waiting until the holidays to shop is one of the most common (and avoidable) reasons people end up with an unplanned coverage gap.

What Changed This Year: The Enhanced Subsidies Expired

Here's the part that's different from the last few years. The temporarily enhanced premium tax credits that made Marketplace plans more affordable — and that removed the income cap on who could qualify for help — expired as of January 1, 2026. That means for 2027 plans, subsidy rules are reverting closer to the pre-2021 structure, including the return of the so-called "subsidy cliff" for households above roughly 400% of the federal poverty level.

What this means in plain terms: if you qualified for a tax credit in the last couple of years, your actual out-of-pocket premium for 2027 could look noticeably different than what you paid before — even in the same plan, at the same income. This isn't a reason to panic, but it is a real reason not to just auto-renew your current plan without checking the numbers first. A few states are stepping in with their own assistance programs, but Florida is not currently one of them, so Florida shoppers are working under the federal rules as they now stand.

What To Do Before November 1

You can't officially enroll before the window opens, but there's real value in getting ready ahead of time:

  • Know your household income estimate for 2027 — subsidy eligibility is based on projected income, and getting this wrong can mean owing money back at tax time.
  • Check whether your current doctors and prescriptions are still in-network for the plan you're considering — networks change year to year even when the plan name doesn't.
  • Don't assume auto-renewal is your best option. With subsidy rules shifting, the plan that made sense last year may not be the cheapest or best option for 2027.

None of this needs to be figured out alone. Running actual numbers against your specific income, household size, and health needs is exactly the kind of thing that's much easier with someone who does this every day than by guessing from a search result.
