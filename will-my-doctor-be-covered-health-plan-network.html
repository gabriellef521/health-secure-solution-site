Will My Doctor Be Covered? How to Check Before You Pick a Plan

Blog / Individuals & Families

By Gaby Fafard, Licensed Health Insurance Advisor · Fort Lauderdale, FL

Here's a mistake I see every open enrollment. Someone picks a plan because the monthly price looks great, and then finds out in January that their doctor isn't covered or their prescription costs three times what they expected. It's easy to avoid if you check a few things before you enroll.

Why It Matters

Every plan has a network, which is the group of doctors, hospitals and labs it has agreed rates with. See someone inside the network and you pay the plan's normal rates. See someone outside it, and you can pay a lot more, or on some plans nothing is covered at all except emergencies. Networks and drug lists can also change from year to year, so a plan that worked for you last year might not this year.

Step 1: Know Your Plan Type

The type of plan tells you how strict the network is. An HMO generally covers care inside its network and often asks you to get a referral to see a specialist. An EPO also stays inside the network but usually doesn't require referrals. A PPO lets you see out-of-network providers, though it costs more. A POS plan is a mix of the two. If you want the most freedom to choose your doctors, that's worth weighing against the price.

Step 2: Look Up Your Doctors By Plan Name

Use the plan's provider directory to search for each of your doctors, specialists and your preferred hospital. Search by the exact plan name, not just the insurance company. One company can offer many plans with different networks, so a doctor can be in one and out of another.

Step 3: Call The Doctor's Office

Directories aren't always up to date. Call each office and ask, "Do you accept this exact plan?" and give them the plan name. It takes a couple of minutes and can save you a big surprise.

Step 4: Check Your Prescriptions

Every plan has a drug list, called a formulary, that shows which medications are covered and how much they cost. Look up each prescription you take. Check whether it's covered, what tier it's in, and whether the plan requires approval before it will pay for it. If you take an expensive medication, this step matters as much as the premium.

Don't Just Auto-Renew

Plans change their networks, drug lists and prices every year. If you're staying on the same plan, it's still worth a quick re-check, because the plan you had last year may not be the same plan this year.

Mind The Deadlines

Open enrollment for 2027 runs November 1 through January 15. Select a plan by December 15 for coverage that starts January 1, or by January 15 for coverage that starts February 1. Don't wait until the last week to check your doctors, because if one isn't covered you'll want time to compare other options.

The short version: before you pick a plan, check your doctors, your hospital and your prescriptions. The cheapest premium isn't a bargain if it doesn't cover the people and medications you rely on.
